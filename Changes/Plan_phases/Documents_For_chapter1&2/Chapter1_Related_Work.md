# INTRODUCTION

## Background of the Study

Managing financial records and monitoring tax compliance obligations for multiple client businesses has become an increasingly complex responsibility for bookkeeping professionals worldwide. As businesses continue transitioning toward digital financial operations, the demand for organized and centralized bookkeeping systems also continues to increase. Studies have shown that the absence of structured financial record management systems negatively affects the accuracy, consistency, and operational efficiency of financial processes within organizations. Without centralized digital platforms, bookkeeping professionals often rely on manual and paper-based methods that are prone to inefficiencies, delayed information retrieval, record inconsistencies, and compliance-related errors. In response to these operational challenges, many organizations and financial professionals worldwide have adopted web-based financial management systems to improve record organization, accessibility, and monitoring processes.

In the Philippines, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) continues to promote the digitalization of tax administration through systems such as the Electronic Filing and Payment System (eFPS), eBIRForms, and the Online Registration and Update System (ORUS) to modernize tax filing and payment processes nationwide. These government initiatives demonstrate the increasing adoption of digital technologies in financial and compliance-related operations. However, despite the availability of these platforms, many bookkeeping professionals who manage multiple client businesses still rely heavily on disconnected and manual workflows in handling financial records, compliance monitoring, and document organization. Existing government systems mainly focus on tax filing and registration processes rather than centralized bookkeeping management and financial monitoring activities performed by independent bookkeepers. As a result, bookkeeping professionals continue to experience difficulties in organizing client records, monitoring financial movement, and maintaining efficient bookkeeping workflows.

Locally, home-based bookkeepers in Panabo City, Davao del Norte commonly manage multiple client businesses simultaneously using manually organized folders, spreadsheets, and physical financial documents. Their bookkeeping activities include recording monthly transactions, organizing financial records, monitoring tax obligations, and assisting clients with BIR-related compliance requirements. Since many client records are manually stored and retrieved through folders or separate files, locating accurate financial information often becomes time-consuming and inefficient, especially when clients have similar business names or multiple business registrations. The absence of centralized client management and TIN-based retrieval mechanisms further increases the difficulty of handling bookkeeping records efficiently during tax preparation and financial monitoring activities. In addition, manually organized bookkeeping practices also limit the ability of bookkeepers to generate organized financial summaries, monitor business performance trends, and identify clients that may require closer financial monitoring and advisory support.

Despite the growing adoption of digital financial systems, many existing bookkeeping and accounting platforms primarily focus on enterprise accounting operations, automated accounting services, or individual business management. Some platforms provide financial analytics and forecasting functionalities, while others mainly support tax filing and compliance processing. However, limited systems specifically address the operational challenges encountered by home-based bookkeeping professionals managing multiple client businesses within localized bookkeeping environments. Furthermore, fewer systems integrate centralized client profile management, TIN-based retrieval, descriptive financial analytics, rule-based predictive forecasting indicators, and client remarks/compliance monitoring within a single web-based bookkeeping platform designed for bookkeeping workflow support. These gaps highlight the need for a more centralized and specialized bookkeeping management system suitable for local bookkeeping professionals.

This study supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure, which promotes the development of innovative and technology-driven solutions that strengthen digital transformation and operational productivity across industries. The study is also aligned with the Davao del Norte State College Strategic Development Plan 2025–2029, which emphasizes the development of technology-based solutions that contribute to community improvement and regional socio-economic development.

To address these challenges, this study proposes SafeBooks: A Web-Based Bookkeeping Platform for Client Transaction Entry Records with Financial Analytics and Rule-Based Predictive Forecasting. The proposed system centralizes client profile management, financial record organization, and tax compliance monitoring into a single secure platform accessible through standard web browsers. The system incorporates TIN-based search functionality for faster and more accurate client record retrieval, while its Financial Analytics Engine generates descriptive summaries, financial trend insights, and rule-based predictive forecasting indicators based on recorded financial data. In addition, the platform implements client remarks and compliance tracking (new, active, separated, closed; filed, pending, late) to support bookkeeping monitoring and follow-up activities. Through these features, SafeBooks aims to improve bookkeeping efficiency, strengthen financial record management practices, and support more organized and data-driven decision-making among bookkeeping professionals.
