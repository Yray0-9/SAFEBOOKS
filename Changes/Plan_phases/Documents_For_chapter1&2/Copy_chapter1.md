# INTRODUCTION

## Background of the Study

Managing client financial records and monitoring tax-related obligations are important responsibilities in bookkeeping practice. As bookkeepers handle multiple client businesses, the amount of financial information they must record, organize, retrieve, and review also increases. These records commonly include sales, expenses, tax-related entries, filing schedules, client details, and supporting notes needed for bookkeeping follow-up. When these records are managed manually or through disconnected files, bookkeepers may experience delays in retrieving information, inconsistencies in record organization, and difficulty monitoring the status of each client. Studies have shown that weak financial record management practices can affect the accuracy, consistency, and efficiency of financial processes within organizations [1].

The increasing use of digital financial systems has encouraged many organizations to adopt web-based tools for recordkeeping, reporting, and monitoring. These tools help improve accessibility, reduce dependence on physical documents, and support faster review of financial information [2]. In the Philippines, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has also continued to promote digital transformation in tax administration through platforms such as the Electronic Filing and Payment System (eFPS), eBIRForms, and the Online Registration and Update System (ORUS) [3]. These initiatives show the growing role of digital technology in tax-related processes. However, these government platforms mainly support filing, registration, and payment activities. They do not directly address the internal bookkeeping needs of professionals who must manage several client records, monitor compliance status, and review financial movement before tax-related activities are completed.

In local bookkeeping practice, especially among bookkeepers who serve several client businesses, record management often involves separate folders, spreadsheets, credentials, printed documents, and manually updated lists. This setup may become difficult to maintain as the number of clients grows. Locating the correct client record can take time, particularly when clients have similar names or when one client owns more than one business. The absence of centralized client management and Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)-based retrieval can further affect the speed and accuracy of record access. In addition, compliance monitoring becomes more difficult when filing status, client activity, deadlines, and remarks are tracked separately from the actual financial records.

Aside from record organization and compliance monitoring, another challenge is the limited use of historical financial data for analysis. Bookkeepers may have access to years of client transaction records, but these records are often used only for basic encoding or manual review. Without an analytics component, it is difficult to summarize sales, expenses, tax-related records, and net values across periods. Without a forecasting methodology, future financial movement cannot be evaluated in a measurable way. For forecasting to be defensible, the system must not simply display projected values; it must compare forecasting models using accuracy metrics and select the model with the best performance. In this study, Moving Average, Weighted Moving Average, and Linear Regression are considered as candidate forecasting models, and their performance is evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). Based on the evaluation, the selected forecasting model is integrated into the system for frequency-aware forecasting according to monthly, quarterly, or annual transaction schedules.

Despite the availability of accounting and bookkeeping applications, many existing systems are designed for general business accounting, enterprise operations, or direct tax filing support. Some systems provide reporting and analytics, while others focus mainly on compliance processing. However, fewer systems combine centralized client financial records, compliance monitoring, descriptive financial analytics, forecasting model evaluation, and report outputs in one workflow suited for bookkeeping record management. This gap highlights the need for a system that can support the day-to-day work of organizing client records while also providing useful financial summaries and forecasting analytics.

This study supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure, which promotes innovation and the development of technology-driven solutions that strengthen productivity and digital transformation [7]. The study is also aligned with the Davao del Norte State College Strategic Development Plan 2025-2029, which emphasizes technology-based solutions that contribute to community improvement and regional socio-economic development [8].

To address these challenges, this study proposes SafeBooks: A Web-Based Financial Records and Compliance Monitoring System with Forecasting Analytics. The system centralizes client profiles and related bookkeeping records, supports structured period-based entry of sales, expenses, tax-related items, deadlines, and notes, and provides monitoring tools for client activity, filing status, remarks, and upcoming compliance deadlines. It also generates financial analytics that summarize recorded financial data and present trends for bookkeeper review. For forecasting, the study trains, tests, and compares Moving Average, Weighted Moving Average, and Linear Regression models using historical client financial records, then integrates the selected Weighted Moving Average model into the analytics module. Through these functions, the system aims to improve record organization, support compliance monitoring, and provide data-driven financial insights for bookkeeping review and follow-up.
